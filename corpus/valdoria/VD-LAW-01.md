# Valdoria e-Invoicing Act (consolidated text)

## 1. Subject and definitions
This Act introduces the mandatory clearance of electronic invoices in the Republic of Valdoria. For the purposes of
this Act, the **Valdoria Clearance Platform (VCP)** is the information system operated by the Valdorian Revenue Office
that validates invoices before they are issued; a **Clearance ID** is the unique identifier VCP assigns to a cleared
invoice; a **large taxpayer** is a VAT-registered person whose turnover in the previous calendar year exceeded
EUR 10 million.

## 2. Obligation to clear invoices (Article 4)
(1) Invoices for business-to-government supplies shall be cleared through VCP from 1 January 2025.
(2) Invoices for business-to-business supplies issued by large taxpayers shall be cleared through VCP from
1 January 2026.
(3) Invoices for business-to-business supplies issued by all other VAT-registered taxpayers shall be cleared through
VCP from 1 January 2027.
(4) Invoices to consumers are outside the scope of this Act.

## 3. Clearance and issue (Article 5)
The supplier submits the invoice to VCP in the structured format defined by the Revenue Office. VCP validates the
invoice and either returns a Clearance ID or rejects it with the reasons. An invoice is legally issued only when VCP
has returned its Clearance ID. The supplier then sends the invoice to the buyer with the Clearance ID stated on it.

## 4. Rejected invoices (Article 6)
An invoice rejected by VCP has not been issued. The supplier shall correct it and submit it again within five working
days of the rejection.

## 5. Corrections (Article 7)
A cleared invoice may not be modified or cancelled. It is corrected only by a credit note that references the original
invoice and its Clearance ID; the credit note is itself cleared through VCP.

## 6. Penalties (Article 9)
Issuing an invoice without clearance where clearance is mandatory is an administrative offence. The amounts of the
penalties are laid down in a separate regulation of the Ministry of Finance.
